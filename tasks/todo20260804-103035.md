## Review

The branch went through two review rounds. The first was blocking and correct on all six findings;
the central charge — an `n/a` mechanism hiding our own defects — was true and understated (three of
four examined `n/a` entries were didcomm-dotnet bugs). The work chased each to root cause rather
than to the nearest green: the JOSE `kid` saga took two upstream releases (#17 then #25 → 1.3.0),
the ES256K rejection was upstream in NetCrypto (#23 → 1.5.0), and the forward-attachment `id` was
ours. Every fix is pinned by a red-first test, and two of the diagnostic probes had their *own*
bugs caught mid-flight (a wrong group-order constant that clobbered R; an `n/a` that would have been
retired by a lucky green run rather than parity-classified sampling) — which became the strongest
evidence that the verification discipline the first review demanded was actually in place. The
second review confirmed the code work and reduced to edit-only paperwork, now done.
